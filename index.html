Slice doesn't tell you

👤
User Interviews — 2 Participants
Conducted via WhatsApp. Identified a UPI-only user (User 1) and an informed card user who learned billing only from a physical manual (User 2). Correctly separated two distinct user segments and exposed a critical gap — the app never educates users at the payment moment.
📋
Reddit Community Analysis
Reviewed r/IndiaFinance and r/CreditCardsIndia. Identified recurring complaints about minimum due confusion, unexpected interest charges, and GST — all from first-time credit card users aged 19–26.
⭐
Play Store Review Mining
Analyzed 1–2 star reviews from late 2025 to mid-2026. Billing confusion, the slider UI preventing exact payments, and autopay failures were the top recurring themes across hundreds of critical reviews.
User Segments
Confused card users Target segment
Age 19–26. First credit card ever. Pay minimum due assuming it's safe. Get hit with interest and GST on the next statement. This is who this case study is designed for.
Informed card users
Understood billing — but only from a physical card manual received at signup, not from the app. Proves this is a systematic UI failure, not a user knowledge gap. (User 2)
UPI-only users
Use Slice purely as a scanner and balance checker. No credit card interaction. A completely different problem. Excluded from this case study scope. (User 1)
Key Findings
1
Minimum due is treated as interest-free: Users select "Pay Minimum Due" assuming the remaining balance carries forward without penalty — like a grace period. The app never corrects this assumption before payment is confirmed.
2
Interest consequence is hidden until it's too late: The app shows no interest rate, no APR, and no projected charge before the user swipes to pay minimum due. Users only discover the consequence on their next statement — after the damage is done.
★
Even informed users rely on external materials, not the app User 2 insight
User 2 understood billing only because of a physical card manual received 6 months ago — not because the app explained it. This proves the problem is a systematic UI failure affecting everyone, not just uninformed users. The app never guides anyone at payment — regardless of their knowledge level.
3
GST on interest creates a second shock: Users hit by interest charges are then surprised by GST applied on top — a second unexpected charge the UI never warns about at any point in the payment flow.
4
The UI physically blocks exact payments: A slider-based payment UI forces rounded amounts (e.g., ₹4,500 or ₹5,000) when a user's actual bill is ₹4,780. Paying the exact amount owed is impossible. Confirmed by multiple Play Store reviews.

02 — Problem Statement
Confirmed across 5 sources
First-time Slice credit card users (age 19–26) treat minimum due as an interest-free grace period because the app never shows the financial consequence before payment — leading to compounding interest charges, unexpected GST, and deep loss of trust in the product. Even users who eventually understand billing learned it from external materials — not from the app itself. This is a systematic UI failure.
Sources: User interviews · r/IndiaFinance · Play Store reviews (late 2025–mid 2026) · Fintech forums

04 — Ideation

3 ideas. 1 chosen.

How might we help first-time Slice credit card users understand the real cost of paying minimum due — before they confirm payment — so they never face an unexpected interest charge?

A
Onboarding education module Rejected
A 3-screen mini tutorial shown once after the first bill is generated. Explains billing cycle, due date, minimum vs full, and interest rate in simple language.
✓ Pros
Sets expectations early, before damage is done
One-time effort
✗ Cons
Users skip tutorials when a bill has just arrived
Doesn't help at the actual payment decision point
Doesn't solve root cause
User impact: medium
Effort: low
Solves root cause: no
B
Consequence preview on billing screen Chosen
A real-time nudge triggered the moment a user taps "Minimum Due" — showing the exact interest amount they will be charged next month in rupees, before they confirm. Also replaces the slider with a numeric input field.
✓ Pros
Intervenes at the exact moment of decision
Shows ₹ consequence — not abstract % APR
No new screens needed
Easy to A/B test cleanly
Directly rooted in user research
✗ Cons
Users who can't afford full may feel anxious
Needs real-time backend interest calculation
User impact: high
Effort: medium
Solves root cause: yes
Why this wins: It solves the problem at the exact moment it occurs. Rohan's core need was "tell me before I pay" — this does exactly that, in rupees (not percentages), at the exact screen where he decides. The slider fix is bundled in because we're already touching the billing screen — two confirmed problems solved for the cost of one sprint.

Smart push notification reminder Rejected

Design Principles
₹
Show rupees, not percentages
Rohan doesn't know what 36% APR means. He understands "₹640 extra next month." Concrete rupee amounts — always.
⏱
Intervene at the moment of decision
Education before the bill arrives is ignored. Regret after the charge hits is useless. The billing screen — right before swipe — is the only moment that matters.
🤝
Inform, don't restrict
If Rohan genuinely can't pay full, the UI should not shame him. Show the consequence clearly, then let him decide. Respect his agency.
🔧
Fix two problems, one sprint
Since we're touching the billing screen anyway, replace the slider with a numeric input — solving the exact-payment bug confirmed by Play Store reviews. No extra cost.

06 — Metrics

How we'd measure success

One primary metric. Two secondary signals. One clean A/B test. Hypothetical — but designed to be real.

📊
Primary Metric
Percentage of new users (0–90 days) who pay full due in their first 3 billing cycles. This directly measures whether the consequence preview changed behaviour — not just awareness.
Target: +25% full payment rate
🎫
Secondary — Support Tickets
Track volume of support tickets tagged "unexpected interest charge" or "surprise billing" per month. A successful feature should reduce this category by at least 30% within 60 days of shipping.
Target: −30% complaint volume
⭐
Secondary — Billing Clarity
Post-payment single-question survey: "Did you understand what you were charged today?" Sent once to new users after their first bill payment. Baseline vs post-launch comparison.
Target: 80%+ respond "yes"
A/B Test Plan

07 — Learnings

What I'd do differently

Honest reflection on the process — what worked, what I'd improve, and what I'd explore next.

01
I'd talk to more Slice card users specifically
Only one of my two interview participants used Slice as a credit card. For a fintech case study, I'd recruit at least 4–5 active card users who have received at least 2 bills. Reddit and Play Store filled the gap — but primary research is always stronger than secondary.
02
I'd usability test the screens with real users before finalising
After designing all 6 screens, I moved straight to metrics — skipping a usability test. I should have shown the billing screen redesign to 2–3 real Slice users and asked: "Walk me through what you'd do here." That would catch whether the warning banner was actually noticed, whether "₹755 extra next month" was understood, and whether the numeric input caused any confusion — before treating the design as final. Designing without user feedback on the screens is the same mistake Slice made with their billing UI.
03
The edge case screen was the most valuable design
Screen 6 — the partial payment live calculator — was the hardest to design and the most revealing. Thinking about users who genuinely can't pay the full amount led to the "inform, not restrict" design principle, which then shaped every other screen. I'd start with edge cases earlier next time.
04
I'd scope the onboarding problem as a future iteration
Research revealed that even informed users learned billing from a physical manual — not the app. A future version of this solution should include a lightweight onboarding moment after the first bill is generated. Not a tutorial — just a one-screen explainer that Rohan sees once, contextually, when it matters.
Next Steps
🧪
Ship to 5% of users first. Before the full A/B test, release to a small cohort (5%) to catch any technical issues with the real-time interest calculation from the backend. Monitor for errors, edge cases, and unexpected user behaviour.
📱
Run the 6-week A/B test. 50/50 split, new users only, 2 full billing cycles. Primary metric: full payment rate. Secondary: support ticket volume and billing clarity survey score.
🔄
Iterate based on results. If full payment rate increases but support tickets don't drop — investigate whether interest calculation accuracy is the issue. If neither moves — revisit whether the consequence preview is visible enough or triggers at the right moment.
📋
Tackle onboarding as Phase 2. Once the billing screen is fixed, address the upstream problem: new users getting their first bill with no context. A one-screen contextual explainer after bill generation — not a tutorial — built on the same "rupees, not percentages" design principle.
